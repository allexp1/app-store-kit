## 1. What Apple itself says gets apps rejected

### "Avoiding common issues" (App Review page)

Source: https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/ (the old /app-store/review/ URL redirects here).

Apple's headline: "On average, **over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness**, which covers crashes, placeholder content, incomplete information, and more."

Apple's list of common issues:

1. **Crashes and bugs** (2.1): test on devices running the latest OS.
2. **Broken links** (2.1, 5.1): "All links in your app must be functional. A link to user support with up-to-date contact information and a link to your privacy policy is required for all apps."
3. **Placeholder content** (2.1).
4. **Incomplete information** (2.1): demo account, special configuration, demo video or hardware, contact info. Some apps need documents: Kids apps with third-party ads, medical hardware clearance, third-party content rights, licences for gambling or VPN.
5. **Privacy policy issues** (5.1): the policy must name the data collected and its uses, cover third-party protection, and explain retention and deletion and how to revoke consent.
6. **Unclear data access requests** (5.1.1(ii)): "All apps submitted to App Store Connect that access user data are required to include a purpose string."
7. **Inaccurate screenshots** (2.3.3).
8. **Substandard user interface** (HIG).
9. **Web clippings, content aggregators, or a collection of links** (4.2).
10. **Repeated submission of similar apps** (4.3).
11. **Copycats** (4.1).
12. **Misleading users** (2.3).
13. **Not enough lasting value** (4.2).
14. **Submitted by incorrect entity** (3, 5.1.1(ix)): banking, crypto, healthcare, gambling and air travel apps must come from the legal entity that provides the service.

The same page says App Review typically reviews "at least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours."

### App Store Transparency Report 2025 (Apple)

Source: https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/

- 9,100,620 app submissions reviewed, 2,093,244 rejected (about 23%), and 387,087 approved after an earlier rejection.
- Rejections by section: **Performance 1,354,418** (section 2, which includes 2.1), Legal 495,673, Design 415,532, Business 283,820, Safety 151,159, Other 4,145. One submission can be rejected under several sections.
- 166,899 apps were removed from the App Store.
- The report does not break rejections down by sub-guideline. The publication date and period are not stated on the page; the period is presumably calendar 2025 (**UNCONFIRMED**).

### Guidelines "Before You Submit" checklist

Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/

Test for crashes; make metadata complete and accurate; keep contact info current; give "an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode"; keep the "backend services … live"; explain non-obvious features and IAPs in the review notes; and (new Oct 16, 2026) "Provide required documentation for apps and features that require regulatory approval." The linked help page "Provide information for a complete review" lists what the notes and attachments should hold; `review-guidelines.md` turns it into the review-notes template.

## 2. The checks

Severity scale:

- **BLOCKER** means App Store Connect refuses the upload or the submission.
- **LIKELY REJECTION** means App Review commonly rejects for it.
- **WARNING** means it is a risk, a best practice, or a future requirement.

The Detect column says **Auto** (a script can decide), **Semi** (a script flags candidates and a human confirms) or **Human** (needs judgment or App Store Connect access).

Shorthand used in the Source column:

- **GL** = https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- **UR** = https://developer.apple.com/news/upcoming-requirements/
- **AR** = https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/

## 3. Things I could not confirm (UNCONFIRMED)

- Which `ITMS-*` upload errors are hard failures today versus warning emails. Apple publishes no list; ITMS-90683 is described only in Apple forum threads.
- Whether a missing launch screen or a missing `UIRequiredDeviceCapabilities` is still rejected by the upload validator rather than at review. The documents say "must".
- The exact Schedule 2 (DPLA) subscription disclosures (Terms of Use/EULA link in the app and metadata). The guideline only points to Schedule 2.
- Whether OTA JavaScript updaters (CodePush, Expo Updates) are allowed. That is governed by DPLA 3.3.x, which I did not read.
- An explicit AI-generated-content labeling rule. None was found in the guidelines (October 16, 2026) or on Apple's Foundation Models acceptable-use page. The AI rules are 5.1.2(i) (third-party AI disclosure and consent) and 1.2 (AI content creation apps, nonconsensual intimate imagery).
- iPhone Duo adoption is not a review requirement. The only dated requirement is screenshots from April 2027.
- The deployment-target conflict: Upcoming Requirements says iOS 13+ (since Sep 9, 2026), while the Submitting page says iOS 15+ from April 2027.
- The period covered by the 2025 Transparency Report, which is not stated on the page.
